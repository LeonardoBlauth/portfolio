# 01 — Portfolio Definition

**Project:** Leonardo Blauth's professional portfolio  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** approved to guide later documents; project scope reconciled September 3, 2026 against published cases on `master`

## 1. Context

Leonardo Blauth has worked professionally with web development since 2021 and presents himself as a **Full Stack Developer**, without highlighting a specific seniority in the main title.

His core professional stack is:

- Vue.js;
- JavaScript and TypeScript;
- Laravel;
- PHP;
- MySQL.

He also has experience or familiarity with Node.js, Python, Git, Linux and Docker. He is expanding his knowledge in AI-assisted development, AI agents, automations, modern architectures, PostgreSQL, Redis, queues and asynchronous processing, and real-time applications.

The portfolio should keep these categories clearly separated. Technologies under study cannot be presented as a professional expertise, and a technology should only change category when there is real experience that supports this change.

Leonardo is studying Software Engineering, expected to complete in 2026. This information may appear as a complementary part of the trajectory, without replacing his professional identity.

## 2. Objective

The main objective is to strengthen Leonardo's presentation for **professional opportunities**, both in Brazil and in international or remote contexts.

The portfolio must allow recruiters and technical leaders to quickly understand:

- who Leonardo is and how he works;
- his professional experience since 2021;
- his main stack and skills;
- the problems it can help solve;
- the projects he develops;
- how he contributes to product and engineering decisions;
- where to deepen professional and technical evidence;
- how to get in touch for opportunities.

Potential customers and partners form a secondary audience. The website may be useful for this group, but should not adopt the language or structure of a commercial service page.

## 3. Audience

### 3.1 Priority audience

- recruiters;
- Tech Leads;
- Engineering Managers;
- other leadership and technical teams.

### 3.2 Secondary audience

- developers;
- companies;
- potential customers or partners.

### 3.3 Reading levels

Content should work on two layers:

1. **Quick read:** understandable positioning, experience, stack, projects and contact without in-depth technical knowledge.
2. **In-Depth:** case studies, decisions, challenges, alternatives and trade-offs for technical visitors.

## 4. Positioning

### 4.1 Main positioning

**Full Stack Product-oriented Developer, with the ability to understand problems, participate in decisions and develop end-to-end web solutions.**

This positioning must be supported by practical experience in frontend, backend, databases, integrations and automations. Architecture and code quality should appear through concrete decisions and evidence, not blanket statements.

### 4.2 Secondary characteristics

- ability to move between product and implementation needs;
- solving real problems;
- construction and evolution of applications;
- continuous learning;
- technical versatility without claiming universal specialization;
- responsible use of AI as part of a modern engineering workflow.

### 4.3 Positioning limits

The portfolio must not:

- assign a seniority that has not been defined;
- present Leonardo as an expert in all the technologies mentioned;
- confusing study with professional experience;
- invent projects, metrics, impacts, customers or knowledge;
- using technical complexity as a self-promotion resource without real need;
- attribute decisions or knowledge to the AI ​​that Leonardo cannot explain.

## 5. Value proposition and key message

### 5.1 Value proposition

Present a Full Stack developer who combines product vision and technical execution to transform business problems and needs into complete web solutions, with attention to software clarity, maintenance and evolution.

### 5.2 Main working message

> Full Stack Developer who understands the problem, participates in decisions and develops end-to-end web solutions.

This sentence defines the central idea, but is still not the definitive text of Hero. The final wording, tone and English version will be defined later, together with the architecture and content of the interface.

### 5.3 Evidence that must support the message

- continuous professional activity since 2021;
- experience summarized at DealerUp;
- clearly identified professional stack;
- public and non-confidential deliverables related to web products, sales processes, surveys, customer service, or initial contact, dashboards, interfaces, integrations, bots and automations;
- evolution of movune from product definition to its future stages;
- additional projects only after they exist and reach sufficient quality.

## 6. Relationship with LinkedIn and GitHub

Each channel will have its own function:

| Channel | Main function |
| --- | --- |
| LinkedIn | Career, professional history, training and more detailed experience. |
| Portfolio | Positioning, professional narrative, skills, project selection and case studies. |
| GitHub | Publicly available technical evidence, documentation, code when publishable, and development history. |

The portfolio should not fully replicate LinkedIn or be limited to a collection of links to repositories. External links should serve as deepening paths.

## 7. Content strategy

### 7.1 Essential content

- identification of Leonardo Blauth as a Full Stack Developer;
- short professional presentation;
- summary of experience since 2021;
- stack and skills organized by nature of experience;
- featured projects;
- access to case studies;
- links to GitHub and LinkedIn;
- contact via email and LinkedIn;
- public location limited to Brazil;
- training in Software Engineering when relevant;
- professional photo;
- configurable availability status.

### 7.2 Professional experience

The Home must present DealerUp, the period since 2021, the role, the main stack, responsibilities and a short selection of relevant deliverables.

Content must remain public and professional. The following cannot be published:

- proprietary architecture or code;
- credentials, customers or internal data;
- unauthorized internal metrics;
- sensitive business rules;
- apparently internal names or details without confirmation;
- information whose publication authorization is doubtful.

DealerUp proprietary projects will not be transformed into case studies. In case of doubt, the information must remain off the website until confirmed.

### 7.3 Technologies and skills

The presentation should avoid a “wall of logos” without context. The content must differentiate:

1. **Main professional stack:** Vue.js, JavaScript/TypeScript, Laravel, PHP and MySQL.
2. **Experience or contact:** Node.js, Python, Git, Linux and Docker.
3. **Under study or exploration:** AI applied to development, agents, modern architectures, PostgreSQL, Redis, queues, asynchronous processing and real-time applications.

Competencies can be described by capability — for example, frontend, backend, data, integrations, and automations — as long as each statement is supported by actual history.

### 7.4 Projects and case studies

The portfolio can launch with one main project or grow to several. Quantity is not a quality criterion.

**movune** was the initially confirmed case and remains published:

- B2B SaaS for managing Physiotherapy and Pilates clinics in Brazil;
- personal project;
- currently in product definition and prototyping;
- publicly discussable only with information pertaining to the project itself.

Additional published cases now on `master` (reconciled September 3, 2026):

- **Rigset** — developer-tool / open-source workstation concept, documented as planned;
- **Overtime automation** — automation concept around schedule-aware WhatsApp reply decisions, documented as concept.

Each case must honestly represent its existing phase. While there is no implementation, it should not suggest that production architecture, code, integrations or results already exist beyond what that case documents.

Each case study will only use blocks that add value, including:

- context and problem;
- objectives and scope;
- role and responsibilities;
- process and evolution;
- existing solution and features;
- screenshots or demonstration;
- technologies actually used;
- decisions, alternatives and trade-offs;
- challenges and learning;
- current state;
- links to demo and GitHub when available.

New projects in the areas of AI, agents, automation, real time, queues, Python, APIs, architecture or open source will only be published after they exist and undergo a quality and relevance assessment.

### 7.5 Lab/Playground

The architecture should support a Lab or Playground in the future, but no public sections or navigation will be displayed at launch until there are relevant experiments.

Planned content, basic studies or placeholders should not be presented as completed projects.

### 7.6 Résumé

The site may offer a PDF resume if there is clear value and a simple process to keep it up to date. Downloading is not a mandatory requirement for the first launch and should not create an outdated or conflicting version with LinkedIn.

## 8. High-level structure

### 8.1 Home

The Home must contain, without definitive visual order at this stage:

- Hero with positioning and main call;
- presentation or About;
- summarized professional experience;
- contextualized skills and technologies;
- featured projects;
- contact;
- complementary links and Footer.

The quantity and final order of the sections will be decided in document `02`. Clarity and impact will take priority over automatic maintenance of all initially imagined sections.

### 8.2 Featured Projects

The structure supports a horizontal multi-project experience with individual prominence. The approved implementation on `master` publishes multiple real projects through a carousel; empty placeholder slides remain excluded.

Detailed carousel behavior and responsive adaptation are recorded in the visual-direction document and the current handoff.

### 8.3 Case study page

Each relevant project may have its own route and a modular composition, allowing sections to be included or omitted depending on the amount of actual content. This structure should allow new projects to be added without restructuring the Home or creating artificially uniform pages.

### 8.4 Future structures

- index or page of all projects, when the quantity justifies;
- Lab / Playground, when there is relevant content;
- downloadable resume, if approved;
- complementary technical content, only if there is sufficient strategy and material.

## 9. Functional requirements

### 9.1 Navigation and content

- allow clear navigation through the Home and case studies;
- allow direct access to each published project;
- offer visible paths to GitHub, LinkedIn and contact;
- open external links in a predictable and identifiable way;
- correctly represent the status of each project;
- allow adding new projects and case blocks without changing the main structure;
- hide sections without content, instead of using placeholders like “coming soon”.

### 9.2 Featured Projects

- support interaction by mouse, trackpad, touch and keyboard when there is horizontal navigation;
- allow swiping on mobile;
- clearly indicate the existence of other projects;
- have understandable and accessible controls;
- do not use autoplay without strong justification;
- adapt to the case of there being only one project published;
- keep all projects discoverable even with JavaScript or reduced motion.

### 9.3 Languages

- offer Portuguese and English;
- maintain complete parity for all published content, including case studies;
- use Portuguese as the initial default language;
- allow manual language switching;
- remember the preference chosen by the visitor;
- do not define the language exclusively by geographic location;
- allow changing the default language strategy in the future.

### 9.4 Theme and preferences

- offer dark mode and light mode designed as intentional experiences;
- allow theme selection and consider system preference;
- remember visitor preference when applicable;
- respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### 9.5 Contact and availability

- prioritize contact for professional opportunities;
- initially use email and LinkedIn, without a form;
- do not require backend, CAPTCHA or anti-spam mechanisms in the first scope;
- use real professional email only after its definition;
- allow the availability status to be activated, changed, or removed easily;
- allow a résumé to be added later without changing the main architecture.

## 10. Non-functional requirements

### 10.1 Accessibility

- coherent semantic structure and hierarchy;
- full keyboard navigation;
- visible focus;
- adequate contrast in both themes;
- relevant alternative texts;
- compatibility with screen readers;
- interactive areas with appropriate dimensions;
- understandable content without relying solely on color, animation or gesture;
- full experience with reduced or disabled movement.

### 10.2 Responsiveness

- serve desktop, notebook, tablet and smartphone from planning;
- adapt composition, priority and interaction to mobile, without just compressing the desktop;
- preserve reading, navigation, contact and project discovery in any viewport;
- Handle horizontal design experience specifically for touch and small screens.

### 10.3 Performance

- prioritize good Core Web Vitals and mobile experience;
- optimize images, fonts and content loading;
- limit JavaScript sent to the client to what is necessary;
- use lazy loading appropriately;
- avoid animations or effects with disproportionate cost;
- maintain good experience on less powerful devices and connections.

### 10.4 SEO and sharing

- allow own metadata per language and per case study;
- consider technical SEO, Open Graph, sitemap and consistent URLs;
- allow sharing of projects with appropriate title, description and image;
- prevent duplicate content or incorrect indexing across languages.

### 10.5 Quality and maintenance

- keep the content separate from the presentation whenever this simplifies updates;
- allow synchronized review of the Portuguese and English versions;
- favor simple, explainable solutions that are compatible with individual maintenance;
- predict testing, lint, formatting, CI/CD and accessibility and performance validations in the implementation;
- do not add dependencies or services without verifiable benefit.

### 10.6 Privacy and security

- publish only personal data necessary for professional presentation;
- limit public location to Brazil;
- do not expose credentials, internal information or sensitive data;
- avoid collecting data without a defined purpose;
- evaluate any analytics tool before adoption, considering need and privacy.

## 11. Initial scope and out-of-scope items

### 11.1 Included

- Bilingual home;
- summarized professional experience;
- categorized skills and stack;
- presentation of movune;
- structure for movune case study;
- links to GitHub and LinkedIn;
- contact via email and LinkedIn;
- professional photo;

- configurable availability;

### 11.2 Conditional

- resume in PDF;
- movune demo and code links when actually available;
- analytics, after defining purpose and privacy approach.

### 11.3 Excluded from the First Release

- contact form and associated backend;
- Lab/Playground without enough content;
- case studies of DealerUp's proprietary products;
- future projects not yet developed;
- blog or technical publication without real strategy and content;
- visual or technical features added only to demonstrate complexity.

## 12. Success criteria

The portfolio will be considered successful when:

1. a visitor quickly understands that Leonardo is a Full Stack Developer, has been working since 2021 and combines product vision with technical execution;
2. recruiters are able to identify experience, main stack, highlighted project and contact without mandatory in-depth analysis;
3. technical visitors find real evidence and decisions in case studies, without inflated content;
4. professional technologies, technologies with experience or contact and technologies under study are clearly differentiated;
5. movune is presented according to its current stage, without anticipating implementation or results;
6. LinkedIn, portfolio and GitHub function as complementary sources;
7. all published content has parity in Portuguese and English;
8. essential functions operate via keyboard, touch and mouse and remain usable with reduced movement;
9. the experience is consistent on mobile and desktop, in light and dark themes;
10. the website presents good performance, adequate technical SEO and lack of unnecessary complexity;
11. new projects can be added without relevant structural reformulation;
12. no unauthorized personal, professional or corporate information is published.

The professional value of the portfolio can later be monitored by signs such as relevant contacts, mentions of the website in selection processes and access to case studies. Artificial metrics should not be established before there is a real base of use.

## 13. Principles for next steps

- content and evidence before visual effects;
- clarity before number of sections;
- depth proportional to the actual material of each project;
- accessibility, responsiveness and performance from conception;
- movement with purpose and reduced alternative;
- AI as an engineering tool, with understood and explainable decisions;
- gradual evolution of the portfolio as new projects and evidence exist;
- prior validation before publishing any potentially confidential information.

Before consolidating `02 — Visual Direction and Architecture`, the following must be discussed and approved:
